# Wheat, Cotton, and Maize Cultivation

## WHEAT CULTIVATION

### Overview
Wheat is India's second most important cereal crop after rice. Mainly grown in North India (Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh) with some cultivation in other states.

### Climate and Season
- **Temperature**: 15-25°C optimal; Rabi (winter) crop: October-March
- **Rainfall**: 60-100 cm; grows well in moderate rainfall areas
- **Soil**: Loam, clay loam, well-drained; pH 6.0-7.5

### Key Cultivation Points
- **Seed rate**: 100 kg/ha for timely sowing; increase to 125 kg/ha for late sowing
- **Spacing**: Row spacing 20-23 cm
- **Water**: 40-50 cm total water requirement; critical at flower emergence and grain filling
- **Nutrients**: N 100-120 kg/ha, P 60 kg/ha, K 40-50 kg/ha (varies by soil test)
- **Growth period**: 120-140 days depending on variety
- **Harvesting**: When grain moisture 12-14%; grain hard when bitten

### Common Issues
- **Rust diseases**: Brown rust, Yellow rust in cool moist conditions → use resistant varieties
- **Loose smut**: Seed treatment with fungicide prevents; use disease-free seed
- **Powdery mildew**: Improve air circulation; use resistant varieties
- **Armyworm**: Use IPM; consult local expert for thresholds

### Regional Considerations
- Most suitable for North India plains
- Limited cultivation in South India due to temperature constraints
- Some cultivation in Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh
- Consult state agricultural department for recommended varieties in your region

**Sources**: ICAR Wheat Production Package, PAU Wheat Bulletin

## COTTON CULTIVATION

### Overview
Cotton is India's primary cash crop and natural fiber source. India is world's largest cotton producer. Grown mainly in Gujarat, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, and Karnataka.

### Climate and Season
- **Temperature**: 18-35°C; sensitive to temperature fluctuations
- **Season**: Kharif (summer monsoon) main season: May-October
- **Rainfall**: 60-100 cm; prefers areas with 40-100 cm rainfall
- **Soil**: Well-drained loam, clay loam; pH 5.5-7.5; poor in waterlogged conditions

### Key Cultivation Points
- **Spacing**: 60×45 cm (90 cm x 45 cm in high fertility soil)
- **Seed rate**: 5-7 kg/ha (hybrid cotton); 10-15 kg/ha (desi cotton)
- **Water**: 40-50 cm; critical during flowering and boll formation
- **Nutrients**: N 100-120 kg/ha, P 60 kg/ha, K 40-60 kg/ha (verify with soil test)
- **Growth period**: 165-180 days for most varieties
- **Fertilizer timing**: Apply N in splits; base, 60 days, and 90 days after sowing

### Common Pests (IMPORTANT)
Cotton is susceptible to serious pests; IPM essential:
- **Stem Weevil**: Damage to stems; use resistant varieties
- **Pink Bollworm**: Damage to developing bolls; use resistant/Bt cotton varieties
- **Jassids (Leafhopper)**: Transmit leaf curl virus; vector control important
- **Whiteflies**: Cause yellowing; transmit viruses
- **Mites**: Cause yellowing in dry conditions

**Management**: 
- Use Bt cotton varieties (genetically modified, resistant to certain insects)
- Integrated Pest Management approach
- Regular field monitoring
- Balanced nutrition reduces some pest susceptibility
- Consult local expert for pest management schedule in your region

### Common Diseases
- **Leaf Curl**: Viral; transmitted by whiteflies → control vectors; resistant varieties
- **Angular Leaf Spot**: Bacterial; manage through resistant varieties, crop rotation
- **Powdery Mildew**: Fungal; improves drainage, balanced nutrition

### Harvesting
- **Pick when**: Bolls fully open; fiber white and fluffy (usually 150-160 days after flowering)
- **Method**: Hand picking (preserves quality) or mechanical harvesting (quicker)
- **Moisture**: Bolls should be dry before picking
- **Timing**: Pick every 7-10 days to avoid leaf contamination

### Regional Considerations
- Different states have preferred varieties
- Pest pressure varies by region
- Consult your state agricultural university for recommended varieties and pest management

**Sources**: ICAR Cotton Production, TNAU Cotton Bulletin

## MAIZE (CORN) CULTIVATION

### Overview
Maize is versatile crop grown for grain, fodder, and industrial uses. Grown across India in varied agro-climatic zones. India is fifth largest maize producer.

### Climate and Season
- **Temperature**: 15-32°C optimal; sensitive at flowering
- **Seasons**: 
  - Kharif (Monsoon): June-October (main season in most areas)
  - Rabi (Winter): November-March (only in irrigated areas)
  - Summer: March-June (only in high-rainfall areas)
- **Rainfall**: 50-75 cm; tolerates drought better than rice
- **Soil**: Well-drained loam, sandy loam; pH 6.0-7.5

### Key Cultivation Points
- **Seed rate**: 20 kg/ha (hybrid); 30-40 kg/ha (open-pollinated)
- **Spacing**: 60 cm x 20 cm (row x plant); = 80,000-100,000 plants/ha
- **Water**: 40-50 cm; critical at flowering/silking and grain filling stages
- **Nutrients**: N 120-150 kg/ha, P 60 kg/ha, K 40-60 kg/ha (soil test recommended)
- **Growth period**: 90-140 days depending on variety and season
- **Application**: Apply nitrogen in 2-3 splits; basal, 30 days, 45 days after sowing

### Common Pests
- **Stem Borer**: Most serious pest; larvae bore into stems → use resistant varieties; IPM
- **Fall Armyworm**: Polyphagous pest; feed on leaves, cobs; management with IPM
- **Leaf Hopper**: Vector of mosaic viruses → control vectors
- **Aphids**: Feed on sap; transmit viruses

**Management**: Use resistant/Bt maize varieties; IPM; regular scouting; balanced nutrition

### Common Diseases
- **Maize Rust**: Brown, Common, Stripe rust → use resistant varieties
- **Leaf Spot**: Cause by Helminthosporium and other fungi → resistant varieties
- **Downy Mildew**: Seed transmission; use disease-free seed, resistant varieties
- **Maize Mosaic/Streak Virus**: Transmitted by insects → vector control; resistant varieties

### Harvesting
- **Grain**: When grains are hard (milk stage past); 12-14% moisture for safe storage
- **Fodder**: Cut before silking or at soft dough stage for animal feed
- **Cob**: After grain removal for industrial use
- **Timing**: 30-40 days after silking for grain maturity

### Variety Selection
- Hybrid maize varieties give higher yield than open-pollinated
- Different varieties for different seasons/regions
- Seed companies provide region-specific varieties
- Consult agricultural department for recommended varieties

### Regional Considerations
- Kharif season optimal in monsoon regions
- Rabi cultivation limited to irrigated areas
- Different pest pressure by region
- Some states give subsidy for good varieties

**Sources**: ICAR Maize Production, PAU/TNAU Maize Bulletin

## Common Questions

**Q: Which crop should I choose for my farm?**
A: Consider: Your region's climate and rainfall pattern, available water for irrigation, soil type, market demand, your experience with crop. Consult local agricultural extension office for region-specific recommendations suitable for your conditions.

**Q: What is the best pest management for cotton?**
A: Bt cotton varieties (insect-resistant) are widely recommended. Use Integrated Pest Management: regular monitoring, balanced nutrition, removal of affected plants, conservation of natural enemies. Avoid unnecessary pesticide use. Consult local expert for specific pest management calendar for your region.

**Q: What is the difference between open-pollinated and hybrid varieties?**
A: Hybrid varieties give higher yield and more uniform crop; seed must be purchased each year. Open-pollinated varieties can be saved for next year; yield is lower but acceptable. Hybrids cost more initially but give better returns. Choice depends on market, resources, and tradition.
